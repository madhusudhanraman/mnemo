# Idle energy work

Status as of 20 October 2026, handed off from the work Mac. Work is on the branch `idle-energy`.

## Goal

Mnemo is always on screen, so its idle cost matters more than its peak cost. The aim is to cut the energy used while nobody is touching him, without making him look lifeless.

## How to measure

```sh
node prototype/build.mjs && sh desktop/build.sh
sh desktop/tools/measure-energy.sh /tmp/mnemo-energy.txt 300
python3 desktop/tools/summarise-top.py /tmp/mnemo-energy.txt $(cat /tmp/mnemo-energy.txt.pids) --from 100
```

The script relaunches the app, finds its three WebKit helper processes (GPU, WebContent, Networking), and samples them with `top` every 2 seconds. Leave the ghost alone while it runs: hovering or dragging switches him to 30 fps.

Caveats:

- `top`'s CPU and energy-impact figures are estimates, not watts. Identical runs differ by about ±0.6% CPU, and awake figures rise when the rest of the machine is busy. True power needs `sudo powermetrics`, which has not been run.
- `top`'s IDLEW column is a running total. Wakeups per second are the difference across the window, not the column value.
- Most of the cost is in the WebKit helpers, not the Mnemo process, so Activity Monitor's Mnemo row understates it.

## Findings (before this branch)

Two-minute idle averages on the work Mac (MacBook Pro, ProMotion display), with the ghost awake:

| State | Total CPU (one core) | Energy impact | Wakeups/s |
|---|---|---|---|
| Animating at 20 fps (default) | ≈8.5% | ≈10 | ≈35–55 |
| Motion paused | 1.0% | 1.2 | 5 |
| Ghost hidden | 0.7% | 0.8 | 2 |

Varying the idle frame rate with the debug settings below:

| Idle fps | 20 | 15 | 10 | 5 | 4 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Total CPU | 8.5% | 8.0% | 8.0% | 5.0% | 4.0% | 2.8% |

- Lowering the frame rate from 20 to 10 saves almost nothing. A temporary frame counter confirmed the page really ran at 19.8 and 10.1 fps. Cost only falls clearly below about 5 fps.
- Pixel ratio 1.5 instead of 2 made no difference, so GPU fill rate isn't the bottleneck.
- Profiling WebContent puts a frame at about 0.5 ms of CPU, split between the JavaScript update and handing the canvas to the compositor. The plateau above 10 fps looks like the fixed cost of keeping the display pipeline and CPU awake at all, not the frames themselves. That reading is plausible but unproven.
- Auto colour sampling adds no measurable cost.
- The Swift shell is not a problem: its main thread is about 99% idle.

Conclusion: frame-rate or resolution tweaks are not worth it. The win is to stop rendering when nothing needs to move.

## What this branch changes

1. **Settle when sleepy.** After 60 seconds without interaction (the existing sleepy threshold), once he is fully drowsy and no gesture, reminder, drag or hover is active, his motion slows to a halt over about 4 seconds. Rendering then stops. Implemented as a `stillness` value from 0 to 1 that scales how fast scene time advances, so motion freezes smoothly with no pose jump.
2. **Breathing float.** While settled, the canvas keeps a slow Web Animations float: 4 px rise and fall, 0.7° sway and a slight squash, on a 5.6-second cycle. The compositor runs it without redrawing the scene, at about 0.1% CPU. The owner rejected complete stillness as lifeless.
3. **Stirs.** Every 20–30 seconds while dozing, he half-wakes for about 3.2 seconds. Scene time runs at 2.4× his sleepy pace, which is about awake speed, and a faint Whirl Flutter ripple (strength 0.3) passes through the sheet. He renders at 20 fps during a stir, then settles again over about 2 seconds. The strength was raised twice at the owner's request.
4. **Waking.** Hover, click, drag or a due reminder resets `lastInteraction`. The float eases out over 0.45 seconds and normal animation resumes. **Pause / resume motion** and Reduce Motion disable the float and stirs.
5. **Debug overrides**, read once at launch and ignored when out of range:
   ```sh
   defaults write local.mnemo.companion debugIdleFPS -float 12      # 1–60, default 20
   defaults write local.mnemo.companion debugSleepyFPS -float 5     # 1–60, default 10
   defaults write local.mnemo.companion debugPixelRatio -float 1.5  # 0.5–2, default min(devicePixelRatio, 2)
   defaults delete local.mnemo.companion debugIdleFPS               # remove an override
   ```
6. **Measurement tools** in `desktop/tools/`.

Code: `prototype/src/index.template.html` (search `stillness`, `doze`, `stir`) and `debugRendering()` in `desktop/Sources/main.swift`. `prototype/index.html` and `ghost.html` are generated by `prototype/build.mjs`.

## Results with this branch

Five-minute run, final version, awake for the first 60 seconds:

| Phase | Total CPU | Energy impact |
|---|---|---|
| Awake, 0–60 s (busy machine) | 11–14% | — |
| Settled, no stir (earlier still-only build) | 0–0.8% | — |
| Settled with float only | 0.1% | 0.1 |
| **Dozing with float and stronger stirs, 100–300 s average** | **4.2%** | **5.1** |

Stirs show as 5–8% spikes in 10-second windows, with near 0% between them. The stronger, longer stirs at 20 fps cost noticeably more than the first gentle version, which averaged about 1.5%.

## Open questions and next steps

- **Look and feel, owner's call:** check stir strength, length (3.2 s), interval (20–30 s) and ripple strength (0.3) by eye. The relevant constants sit together near `stirRipple()` in the template.
- **Cost of stirs:** 4.2% dozing average is about half the awake cost. Options if that's too high: stirs every 30–45 s, shorter stirs, or render stirs at 15 fps. Ask before changing how he looks.
- **Unexplained early window:** in one run, the first minute after settling stayed at 5–9% instead of dropping between stirs. Not reproduced cleanly; worth one more look with the frame counter idea (log `fps`, `stillness` and `stirring` every 10 s through `scriptError`).
- **Awake cost** is unchanged at about 8–10%. A larger idea is to settle sooner, or to settle between gestures while awake. That's a design decision for the owner.
- **Not yet done:** `sudo powermetrics` for true power, and a battery or Low Power Mode adaptation in Swift.
- **Review:** this branch has not been reviewed. A second-model review of `index.template.html` changes is welcome, especially the interaction between `stillness`, `stirEnd` and the 2.4× time gain when he is woken mid-stir.

## Per-machine notes

- Auto colour needs Screen Recording permission, which macOS ties to the code signature. Ad-hoc builds lose it on every rebuild. Each Mac needs its own self-signed code-signing certificate named `Mnemo Local Development`, made with `/System/Library/CoreServices/Certificate Assistant.app`. On macOS 27, Keychain Access no longer has the Certificate Assistant menu. If `security find-identity -v -p codesigning` doesn't list it (an untrusted self-signed certificate won't be listed), put its SHA-1 hash from `security find-identity -p codesigning` into the git-ignored `desktop/signing-identity.local`. Then grant Screen Recording once.
- The built app is `desktop/build/Mnemo.app` inside the clone. Nothing copies it to `~/Applications` yet.
